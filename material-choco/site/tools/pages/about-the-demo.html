Open the demo

It opens the same interface the app and the hub use, with a banner that marks it as a demo.

What it is

  • The real interface. The same pages, menus, confirmations and full-screen results as the app.
  • Simulated machines. A pretend hub runs inside the page, with made-up machines and packages. An "install" in the demo changes only that pretend data.
  • Nothing is installed. No Chocolatey command runs anywhere. Your computer is not changed.
  • Nothing leaves the browser. The demo makes no network requests of its own. What you type stays in the page.

What it looks like

A banner at the top of the demo says at all times that the machines are simulated. It has two buttons: one adds a simulated machine, the other resets the demo.

{{frame:47-demo-overview.png}}{{frame:50-demo-machine-joined.png}}

What it cannot show

  • Real results. Output, exit codes and timings in the demo come from the simulation. They show how the app reports results, not how any package behaves.
  • Anything that needs a real hub or a real Windows machine. Real agents, HTTPS, deliveries to other services and real installs.
  • Proof. The demo proves nothing about the app on Windows. For what has and has not been proven, see the status page.
